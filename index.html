Unit 1: Pre-Algebra

1. Order of Operations
Level of Importance: ★★★★★ (MUST KNOW)

The only rule to follow is GEMDAS.

  1. G - Groupings: () then [] then {}
    Note: Sometimes there are only parentheses, follow the INSIDE-TO-OUTSIDE rule.
  2. E - Exponents
  3. M - Multiplication / D - Division: Are in the same level of priority. Prioritize LEFT FIRST.
  4. A - Addition / S - Subtraction: Are in the same level of priority. Prioritize LEFT FIRST.

2. Divisibility Rules
Level of Importance: ★★★★☆ (CONVENIENT TO KNOW)

Definition: A number is divisible by a number n when the remainder is 0 when the number is divided by n.

  • Divisible by 2: If the ones digit is even or among the following: 0, 2, 4, 6, 8.
  • Divisible by 3: If the sum of all digits is divisible by 3.
  • Divisible by 4: If the number formed by the tens and ones digit is divisible by 4.
  • Divisible by 5: If the ones digit is either 0 or 5.
  • Divisible by 6: If the number is divisible by both 2 and 3.
  • Divisible by 7: Double the ones digit, subtract it from the remaining digits. If the difference is divisible by 7, the number is divisible by 7.
  • Divisible by 8: If it is even and the number formed by the hundreds, tens, and ones digit is divisible by 8.
  • Divisible by 9: If the sum of all the digits is divisible by 9.
  • Divisible by 10: If the number ends in, or the ones digit is, 0.

3. Exponents
Level of Importance: ★★★★★ (HELPS IN MOST CASES)
  • Zero Exponent: Any number EXCEPT 0 raised to 0 is equal to 1. (Note: 00 → INDETERMINATE)
    Examples: 30 = 1 | 170 = 1 | 5230 = 1
  • Base 1: 1 raised to any number equals 1.
    Examples: 14 = 1 | 10 = 1 | 12017 = 1
  • Exponent 1: Any number raised to 1 is itself.
    Examples: 71 = 7 | 581 = 58
  • Negative Exponents: Negative exponents mean reciprocal.
    Examples: 5-1 = 1/5 | 2-3 = 1/8
  • Multiplying Same Base: Add exponents when multiplying numbers with the same base.
    Examples: 32 × 33 = 35 | 124 × 126 = 1210
  • Dividing Same Base: Subtract exponents when dividing numbers with the same base.
    Examples: 33 ÷ 32 = 31 = 3 | 126 ÷ 124 = 122
  • Same Exponents: To multiply or divide numbers with the same exponents, simply do the corresponding operation on their bases and keep the exponents.
    Examples: 33 × 63 = 183 | 214 ÷ 74 = 34
  • Power of a Power: Multiply exponents raised to exponents.
    Example: (53)5 = 515
  • Distributing Exponents: Distribute exponents outside of groupings.
    Examples: (7x)3 = 73x3 = 343x3 | (1/2)4 = 14 / 24 = 1/16
  • Fractional Exponents: If the exponent is a fraction, place the base under a radical with index of the denominator and exponent of the numerator.
    Examples: 21/2 = √2 | 123/4 = 4√(123)

4. Fractions
Level of Importance: ★★★★★ (FUNDAMENTAL)

Relationships of Fractions

  • Same Denominator: The one with the greater numerator is greater.
    Example: 11/72 > 7/72
  • Same Numerator: The one with the lesser denominator is greater.
    Example: 13/3 > 13/4
  • Quick Comparison (Different Numerators & Denominators): Cross multiply the numerators and denominators; numerators take priority in comparison. The numerator that yielded the greater product is the greater fraction.
    Example: Compare 3/13 and 5/27 → (3 × 27) and (5 × 13) → 81 > 65 → 3/13 > 5/27

Operations on Fractions

Definition: Fractions are "like" when they have similar denominators.

Relationships of Fractions

  • Same Denominator: The one with the greater numerator is greater.
    Example

  • Same Numerator: The one with the lesser denominator is greater.
    Example

    Fractions Cross Multiplication Result
    3/13 and 5/27 (3 × 27) and (5 × 13) 81 > 65 → 3/13 > 5/27

Operations on Fractions

Definition: Fractions are "like" when they have similar denominators.

  • Addition or Subtraction:
    Step 1: Turn fractions into like fractions. To turn unlike fractions into like fractions, you may choose any non-zero number to multiply or divide both the numerator and denominator of a fraction to have both fractions with similar denominator.
    Step 2: Add or subtract the numerators according to the corresponding operation on fractions.
    Type Example
    Like Denominators 2/5 + 4/5 = 6/5
    Unlike Denominators 7/2 + 8/3 = (7×3)/(2×3) + (8×2)/(3×2) = 21/6 + 16/6 = 37/6
  • Multiplication: Multiply both the numerators with each other and both denominators with each other.
    Examples
    (11/9) × (5/2) = (11×5)/(9×2) = 55/18
    (17/3) × (14/3) = (17×14)/(3×3) = 238/9
  • Division: Get the reciprocal of the divisor then multiply. (Definition: The reciprocal of a fraction a/b is b/a).
    Examples
    (11/9) ÷ (5/2) = (11/9) × (2/5) = 22/45
    (17/3) ÷ (14/3) = (17/3) × (3/14) = 51/42
Concept Rule Examples
Comparing Same denom: Greater numerator is greater. Same num: Lesser denom is greater. Or, cross multiply. 11/72 > 7/72
3/13 vs 5/27 → 81 > 65
Add / Subtract Turn into like fractions (same denominator), then operate on numerators. 7/2 + 8/3 = 21/6 + 16/6 = 37/6
Multiply Multiply numerators together, multiply denominators together. (11/9) × (5/2) = 55/18
Divide Get the reciprocal of the divisor, then multiply. (11/9) ÷ (5/2) = (11/9) × (2/5) = 22/45
